Built by a Practitioner, Not a Guru

I built AI Search Mastery because I needed it myself. As a systems programmer building AI-powered products, I needed my own tools to be discoverable by AI search — and realised most businesses had no idea this was even a problem.

The Story

I'm a systems programmer building AI-powered products as a solo founder. When I started shipping my own tools, I hit a wall: AI search engines like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews were answering questions directly — and my products were invisible to them. Traditional SEO wasn't enough. I needed to understand how AI decides which businesses to recommend.

So I did what any programmer would do — I researched, tested, and built. I dug into how large language models evaluate and rank businesses. The signals AI looks for are different from traditional search: authority, structured data, sentiment, topical depth — factors that no existing tool was measuring. So I built one.

That research became the MASTERY-AI Framework: 8 pillars and 149 atomic factors that shape whether AI recommends your business or ignores it. I built the tools to solve my own problem first. Then I realised every business building online has the same problem.

The Journey

  • Hit the AI visibility wall

    Started shipping AI-powered products as a solo founder and discovered they were invisible to AI search engines — no matter how good the code was.

  • Researched how AI models evaluate and recommend businesses

    Dug into the signals large language models use to decide which brands to mention: authority, structure, sentiment, and more.

  • Built the MASTERY-AI Framework

    Codified the research into a repeatable, measurable system: 8 pillars and 149 atomic factors any business can use.

  • Launched AImpactScanner

    Built the first tool, so a business could see exactly how AI perceived it and what to fix.

  • Launched LLM.txt Mastery

    Built a tool that generated the structured files AI models need in order to understand a business.

  • Switched both tools off

    AImpactScanner and LLM.txt Mastery were retired in August 2026 and no longer run as services. Their source code is public under the MIT licence: AImpactScanner (opens in new tab) and LLM.txt Mastery (opens in new tab). The framework and the writing stay here.

The Mission

Every business with genuine value is discoverable.

AI search is the biggest shift in how customers find businesses since Google launched. The companies that adapt early will thrive. My mission is to make sure that adapting doesn't require a six-figure agency contract or a team of specialists — just a framework you can read and apply yourself.

What I Believe

Radical Transparency

Show the methodology and the limitations, and say plainly when something has been switched off. If it is not transparent, it is not trustworthy.

Practitioner-First

Build from experience, not theory. Every feature existed because I needed it myself.

Relentless Simplicity

If a solopreneur cannot use it in five minutes, it is too complicated.

Evidence Over Opinion

Measure, test, iterate. Claims without data are just marketing.

Accessible Excellence

World-class tools shouldn't require world-class budgets.

That experience led me to build these tools. Two of them ran as paid products and were switched off in August 2026; a third was built and never opened to anyone, and a benchmark I announced never ran a cycle. The whole account is here, and the code for the two that shipped is public.
