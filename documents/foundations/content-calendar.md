## Content Calendar Overview

### Content Themes and Objectives

**Theme 1: AI Search Education (The Shift)**
- Theme focus: Helping business owners understand that AI search is a structural change in how customers discover businesses -- what is happening, why it matters, and whether it affects their specific situation
- Customer value: The reader gains clarity about a problem they may not have known existed and can verify it for themselves in 15 seconds
- Content alignment: Supports Campaign 1 ("The AI Visibility Wake-Up"), serves the Awareness stage of the customer journey
- Publication frequency: 2-3 blog posts per month (months 1-3), tapering to 1-2 per month (months 4-6). 1-2 LinkedIn posts per week. 1 Twitter/X post per week.

**Theme 2: Practical Optimisation (Fix and Improve)**
- Theme focus: Specific, step-by-step guidance on closing AI visibility gaps -- actionable whether or not the reader uses our tools
- Customer value: The reader can take a concrete action today that improves their AI visibility, with clear instructions and expected outcomes
- Content alignment: Supports Campaign 2 ("Diagnose Your AI Visibility") and Campaign 3 ("From Score to Solution"), serves the Consideration and Decision stages
- Publication frequency: 1 blog post per month (months 1-3), ramping to 2 per month (months 4-6). 1 LinkedIn post per week. 1 Twitter/X post per week.

**Theme 3: Tool Demonstrations (See It In Action)**
- Theme focus: Showing the AImpactScanner and LLM.txt Mastery tools in action -- scan walkthroughs, before/after comparisons, the full Diagnose-Optimise-Test-Repeat loop
- Customer value: The reader sees exactly what the tools do, how they work, and what the output looks like before committing any money
- Content alignment: Supports Campaign 2 and Campaign 3, serves the Consideration and Decision stages
- Publication frequency: 1 blog post every 4-6 weeks. Scan walkthrough video in month 3-4. LinkedIn posts sharing scan findings 1-2 times per week.

**Theme 4: Industry Insights (Data and Trends)**
- Theme focus: Timely analysis of AI search developments, citation pattern changes, platform updates, and original research from scanning real sites
- Customer value: The reader stays informed about a fast-moving landscape without having to monitor it themselves
- Content alignment: Supports Campaign 1 (awareness through authority), serves all journey stages by keeping content current
- Publication frequency: 1 blog post per month (landscape update). 1-2 LinkedIn posts per week with data-driven observations.

**Theme 5: Practitioner Stories (Building in Public)**
- Theme focus: Honest reflections on what we are learning, building, and testing -- including what did not work. FreecalcHub as a live case study. The founder journey in real time.
- Customer value: The reader sees a practitioner who is in the same arena, building the same things, facing the same challenges -- peer credibility, not guru authority
- Content alignment: Supports all campaigns by building trust. Serves Awareness (relatability) and Advocacy (transparency) stages.
- Publication frequency: 1-2 LinkedIn posts per week. 1 blog post every 4-6 weeks ("Optimise in Public" series). Indie Hackers build-in-public updates monthly.

## Content Specifications

### Content Type 1: Blog Posts

**Purpose:**
Educational anchor content that serves all customer journey stages, ranks in traditional search, gets cited by AI search engines, and provides the foundation that all other channel content is derived from. Each blog post is a permanent, compounding asset.

**Format Specifications:**
- Length: 1,200-2,500 words. Awareness posts at the shorter end, methodology and guide content at the longer end.
- Structure: H2 headers every 200-300 words. H3 subheadings within sections where needed. Bullet points for scannable lists. Numbered lists for sequential steps. 3-4 sentence paragraphs maximum. Single CTA at the end, aligned to the post's journey stage.
- Meta description: 150-160 characters, includes primary keyword and a clear value proposition. Optimised for both Google snippet and AI citation.
- Schema markup: Article schema on every post. FAQ schema where appropriate (posts with question-answer format). Author schema linking to Jamie's profile.
- Featured image: Uses brand palette. No stock photography. Screenshots of the product in action, data visualisations, or clean typographic images with the post title on the brand gradient.
- URL structure: jamiewatters.work/journey/[descriptive-slug] -- short, keyword-rich, no dates in URL.
- ICP tag: Every post carries an ICP tag (ICP 1, ICP 2, or ICP 3) in its metadata that determines vocabulary depth.

**Brand Integration:**
- First-person practitioner voice ("I scanned 50 sites this week and here is what I found")
- Specific numbers over vague claims ("27 factors across 8 pillars" not "comprehensive analysis")
- No jargon without explanation at ICP 1 level
- Calm, confident tone -- no exclamation marks in body copy
- Acknowledge uncertainty when appropriate ("AI search is evolving. Here is what we know today.")

**Customer Value:**
Every post must answer: "What does the reader know or can do after reading this that they could not before?" If the answer is "nothing new," the post does not get published.

**Success Metrics:**
- Engagement: Time-on-page (target: 3+ minutes), scroll depth (target: 60%+), comments
- Value: CTA click-through rate to free scan (target: 5-10% of readers), email opt-in rate from content
- Business: Blog-attributed paid conversions (tracked via UTM), organic traffic per post
- SEO: Keyword ranking positions, AI search citation tracking

**Creation Process:**
1. Research (30 min): Identify target keyword, review search intent, check competitor content, gather data points and specific examples
2. Outline (15 min): H2 structure, key points per section, CTA selection, internal links planned
3. Draft (60-90 min): Write in one sitting during Tuesday writing block. First-person voice. Start with the reader's problem. End with a specific next step.
4. Edit (30 min): Wednesday morning. Cut for clarity. Check reading level (8th grade for ICP 1). Verify all statistics have sources. Remove any jargon without explanation.
5. Optimise (15 min): Add meta description, schema markup, internal links, featured image, alt text on all images. Verify URL slug.
6. Publish (10 min): Thursday 9-11am ET. Verify live. Share on social channels.
7. Distribute (15 min): LinkedIn post referencing the blog topic (link in comment). Tweet with key data point. Email notification to subscribers if significant.

## Content Creation and Management

### Content Creation Workflow

**Ideation Process:**

Idea Sources:
- Scan data from real sites (what patterns emerge from scanning 50+ sites?)
- Customer questions and support tickets (what are people confused about?)
- AI search landscape developments (what changed this week/month?)
- Keyword research (what are people searching for?)
- Community conversations (what are people discussing on Reddit, Indie Hackers, LinkedIn?)
- Competitor content gaps (what is nobody else explaining well?)
- Personal experience building and optimising sites (what did I learn this week?)

Idea Evaluation -- every idea must pass three tests:
1. The "So What?" Test: Does this teach the reader something they can use? If the answer is "nothing new," kill it.
2. The Journey Test: Which customer journey stage does this serve? Does it move someone forward?
3. The Unique Value Test: Can I add genuine practitioner perspective to this, or am I just repeating what others have said?

Idea Capture System:
- Keep a running note (Notion, Apple Notes, or plain text file) titled "Content Ideas"
- Format each entry as: [Topic] -- [Journey Stage] -- [Theme] -- [One-sentence hook]
- Review the list every Sunday during batch creation. Select the week's blog topic.
- Move used ideas to "Published" section. Move rejected ideas to "Parked" section with the reason.

**Blog Post Creation Process (Total: ~3.5 hours per post):**

Step 1 -- Research (30 minutes, Tuesday):
- Identify target keyword using Google Search Console data, keyword research, and AI search citation patterns
- Review top 5 ranking articles for the target keyword. Note what they cover well and what they miss.
- Gather 3-5 specific data points, statistics, or scan findings to include
- Check for internal linking opportunities (which existing posts can this link to and from?)

Step 2 -- Outline (15 minutes, Tuesday):
- Write the H2 structure (4-6 H2 sections for a 1,500-word post)
- Note 2-3 key points per section
- Decide the CTA (aligned to journey stage)
- Identify 2-3 internal links to include
- Write the opening line (start with the reader's problem, not our solution)

Step 3 -- Draft (60-90 minutes, Tuesday):
- Write in one sitting. Do not edit while drafting.
- First-person practitioner voice. Specific numbers. Short sentences.
- Include data points gathered in research phase.
- Write the CTA section last.

Step 4 -- Edit (30 minutes, Wednesday):
- Read aloud. Does it sound like Jamie talking to a peer at a conference?
- Cut for clarity. Remove throat-clearing paragraphs. Remove any sentence that does not earn its place.
- Check reading level (8th grade for ICP 1 content). Simplify any sentence over 25 words.
- Verify all statistics have sources. Remove unsubstantiated claims.
- Check for jargon. Define on first use or replace with plain language.
- Verify no exclamation marks in body copy.

Step 5 -- Optimise (15 minutes, Wednesday):
- Write meta description (150-160 characters, includes primary keyword)
- Add schema markup (Article schema, Author schema, FAQ schema where appropriate)
- Add internal links (minimum 2 links to related posts, 1 link to scan page)
- Create or select featured image (brand palette, no stock photos)
- Add alt text to all images
- Set URL slug (short, keyword-rich, no dates)
- Add ICP tag to metadata
- Preview on mobile -- verify readability

Step 6 -- Publish (10 minutes, Thursday 9-11am ET):
- Publish in CMS
- Verify live page renders correctly
- Verify all links work
- Verify schema markup is valid (Google's Rich Results Test)

Step 7 -- Distribute (15 minutes, Thursday):
- Post on LinkedIn (reference the topic, do not repeat the whole post, link in first comment)
- Post on Twitter/X (key data point or finding from the post, link to methodology content when relevant)
- Email subscribers if the post is a significant guide or case study
- Share in relevant Reddit/Indie Hackers threads if topically appropriate

### Content Repurposing Strategy

Every blog post generates 5-8 additional content pieces across channels. This is how a solopreneur maintains 25-30 pieces per month without creating everything from scratch.

**Blog Post to LinkedIn Posts (3-4 posts per blog):**
- Extract the opening hook as a standalone observation post
- Pull one data point or finding as a "here is what I found" post
- Turn the main insight into a practical tip post
- Share a personal reflection or "what surprised me" angle

**Blog Post to Twitter/X (2-3 posts per blog):**
- Key data point as a single tweet
- Main framework or list as a short thread (2-4 tweets)
- Technical methodology angle for SEO community

**Blog Post to Email:**
- Include blog link in monthly update with 1-2 sentence summary
- Reference blog content in nurture sequence emails where topically relevant

**Scan Data to Multiple Channels:**
- One scanning session (50 sites in a niche) generates material for: 1 blog post, 2-3 LinkedIn posts, 1-2 tweets, and outreach email personalisation

**Case Study to Multiple Channels:**
- One customer case study generates: 1 blog post, 2-3 LinkedIn posts (before state, action taken, result), 1-2 tweets, email nurture content, and shareable graphics

### Content Quality Standards

**Value Standards:**

Every piece of content must pass four value tests before publication:

1. Customer value: "What does the reader know or can do after reading this that they could not before?" If the answer is "nothing new," do not publish.
2. Educational value: Does this teach something specific about AI search that the reader can apply -- whether or not they use our tools?
3. Practical value: Does this end with a clear, specific next step? Not "think about your AI strategy" but "scan your site and check whether your structured data is present."
4. Authentic value: Does this reflect genuine practitioner experience? Are we sharing what we actually found, built, tested, or learned?

**Brand Standards:**

1. Voice consistency: Every piece sounds like the same practitioner wrote it. Confident, specific, honest, peer-to-peer. The voice test: read it aloud. Does it sound like Jamie at a conference?
2. Visual consistency: Blog featured images use brand palette. Social graphics use brand gradient. No stock photography. No emojis as UI elements.
3. Value consistency: Clarity Over Cleverness, Honesty Over Hype, Action Over Analysis demonstrated through behaviour, not declared.
4. Personality consistency: Clear, calm, honest, practical, warm but professional. Never corporate, academic, breathless, or guru-like.

**Quality Benchmarks:**

1. Accuracy: All statistics include source and date. All product claims verifiable by running the product. AI search landscape claims caveated ("as of [month] 2026"). MASTERY-AI Framework version number on first reference in methodology content.
2. Clarity: 8th-grade reading level for ICP 1/2 content. No jargon without explanation. Sentences under 25 words. Paragraphs 3-4 sentences maximum.
3. Completeness: Every article covers its topic thoroughly enough that the reader does not need to read three other articles to understand it.
4. Consistency: Brand Style Guide v1.1 voice and tone. Preferred vocabulary from Section 3.4 of the style guide.

### Content Optimisation

**Weekly Review (15 minutes, every Monday morning):**

What to check:
- Last week's blog post performance: pageviews, time-on-page, CTA click-through
- Top-performing LinkedIn post and why
- Scan activations this week (total and by source)
- Any email sequence issues (unusual drop-offs, high unsubscribes)

Actions:

- If a LinkedIn post had high engagement, create a follow-up post exploring the same angle
- If an email had low opens, test a new subject line on the next similar email
- Log one insight in the content performance log

**Monthly Review (1 hour, first Monday of each month):**

What to review:
- Full funnel: traffic > scans > emails > paid > active. Where is the biggest drop-off?
- Channel comparison: which channel drove the most scans? Most paid conversions? Best ROI on time?
- Content type analysis: which blog topics drove the most engagement? Which LinkedIn post types resonated?
- Keyword performance: which target keywords are we ranking for? Which are stalling?
- Email sequence performance: open rates, click rates, conversion rates per email
- Budget vs. actual

Monthly output -- three decisions documented:
1. One thing to double down on (the best-performing content type, topic, or channel)
2. One thing to stop or reduce (the lowest-performing activity relative to time invested)
3. One experiment to try next month

**Quarterly Content Audit (half-day, end of Q1 and Q2):**

What to audit:
- All published blog posts: update outdated information, refresh statistics, improve underperforming posts
- Content gap analysis: which customer journey stages are underserved?
- Keyword gap analysis: which target keywords do we not yet have content for?
- Content theme balance: are all five themes represented proportionally?
- Marketing Bible alignment check: does all content follow the principles?

Actions:
- Identify 3-5 blog posts to update and refresh
- Identify 3-5 content gaps to fill in the next quarter
- Adjust content theme balance for the next quarter
- Update the content calendar for the next 3 months

**A/B Testing Approach:**

One experiment per month. Small, bounded, measurable. Format for each test:

- Hypothesis: "If we [change X], then [metric Y] will [improve/increase] because [rationale]."
- Method: What specifically we are testing, for how long, with what sample size
- Measurement: What metric we are tracking and what constitutes a meaningful result
- Result: What happened
- Learning: What we now know, documented for institutional memory

Planned experiments (one per month):
- Month 1: Scan page CTA language (A/B test "See how AI sees you" vs "Check your AI visibility in 15 seconds")
- Month 2: LinkedIn post type (data-driven scan insights vs observational posts -- 4 of each, compare engagement)
- Month 3: Blog CTA placement (end-of-post only vs end-of-post + mid-article sidebar)
- Month 4: Email nurture timing (current 0-3-7-14-21 day spacing vs compressed 0-2-5-10-17)
- Month 5: LinkedIn post format (text-only vs document/carousel for framework content)
- Month 6: Blog content upgrade (PDF checklist as email opt-in incentive vs no content upgrade)

**Content Update and Refresh Schedule:**

- Posts older than 3 months: Check for outdated statistics, broken links, and AI search landscape changes. Update if necessary.
- Posts older than 6 months: Full content refresh -- update data, add new insights, refresh examples, improve based on performance data.
- Evergreen posts (framework guides, methodology content): Review quarterly. Update when the MASTERY-AI Framework version changes.
- Seasonal/timely posts: Mark as historical if no longer current. Add a note at the top: "This post was written in [month] 2026. The AI search landscape moves fast. Check our latest content for current information."
